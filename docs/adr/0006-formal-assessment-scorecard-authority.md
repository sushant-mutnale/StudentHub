# Formal assessment reports are AI-scored, evidence-backed scorecards

A shared formal assessment report carries an AI score per competency, scored against a published rubric and linked to quoted evidence with confidence and limitations. The candidate may annotate or dispute a score but cannot edit it; the consumer sees the score, rubric, evidence and session-integrity metadata, and no human sign-off is implied. This makes the report useful without presenting it as an identity-verified credential.

**Status:** accepted — report authority for the first integrity tier.

## Consequences

- Rubrics and report schemas must be versioned; scores are only comparable within the same rubric version.
- Evidence extraction becomes a first-class requirement, not a free-text summary.
- Disputes are logged and visible to the consumer; they never silently rewrite the score.
- The report must state its limitations: no proctoring, no identity verification, no human review.
- Sharing lifetime, revocation, retention and deletion must cover the report and its copies.
